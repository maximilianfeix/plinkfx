# plinkfx brand

Everything in `brand/` is generated by one script. To change an asset, change the script and rebuild:

```bash
npm run build            # the banners draw real waveforms through dist/cli.js
python3 brand/make_brand.py
```

Never retouch an SVG or PNG by hand. The colour tokens live in three places that must agree:
this file, the top of `brand/make_brand.py`, and `:root` in `gallery/style.css`.

## 1. Idea

**Sound that lands as a pixel.** The mark is a sound wave drawn on a pixel grid. It starts tall, decays
over 15 columns, and ends in a single orange pixel: the moment the sound lands, the "plink".

It describes the product literally. Every plinkfx sound is a few numbers that decay into a short event,
and the gallery draws each one as a waveform in the same pixel style. Someone who has seen the gallery
recognises the mark, and someone who sees the mark first gets the gallery.

### Three directions, and why this one

| Direction | What it looked like | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| Speaker or sound-wave icon | A speaker cone with arcs, or a smooth sine | Every audio app and every OS volume control already uses it. It says "audio", not "game sounds as code", and disappears next to a system tray. |
| Full chiptune | NES-style palette, a pixel font everywhere, CRT scanlines | It promises 8-bit beeps. The library has chiptune sounds, but also soft UI clicks, footsteps and reverb tails, and the most common criticism of generators is that everything sounds like sfxr. The brand shouldn't confirm that. |
| The gallery grid | A 3×3 grid of tiles with waveforms | Honest to the product, but it turns to mush below 48 px and needs several colours to read. |
| **Decaying pixel wave + one pixel (chosen)** | 15 columns of wave, a gap, one 2×2 orange block | Readable at favicon size, one accent colour, draws directly from the waveform style the gallery already uses, and the orange block gives the name a picture: plink. |

## 2. Logo

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `logo/lockup-light.svg` | Default on light backgrounds: mark + wordmark in ink, "fx" and the drop in plink |
| `logo/lockup-dark.svg` | Default on dark backgrounds: same layout in paper |
| `logo/mark-light.svg` / `logo/mark-dark.svg` | Mark alone: avatars, small spaces |
| `favicon/favicon.svg`, `favicon-32.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png` | The mark centred on a square paper tile, for browser tabs and home screens |
| `banners/social-preview.svg` / `.png` | GitHub social preview, 1280×640 |
| `banners/x-header.svg` / `.png` | X profile header, 1500×500 |
| `banners/readme-light.svg` / `readme-dark.svg` | README header, 1280×400, switched with `prefers-color-scheme` |

The banners show a row of tiles with the real peak envelopes of `coin`, `jump`, `hit`, `laser` and others,
rendered by the CLI at build time. The first tile is the only one in plink.

**Grid.** The mark is built on a square grid: the wave is 15 cells wide and up to 11 cells tall, the drop
is 2×2 cells, one empty column apart. Call one cell **u**. In the lockup the wordmark is drawn on its own
pixel glyphs at 1.2 u per glyph pixel, 4 u to the right of the mark.

**Clear space.** Keep at least 2 u (the width of the drop) free on every side of the mark or lockup. No text,
edge or other logo inside that space.

**Minimum sizes.**

- Mark: 36 px wide on screen (2 px per cell). Below that the steps blur into a smear.
- Lockup: 24 px tall (each wordmark pixel is then about 2.6 px).
- Smaller than that, use the favicon files: the paper tile keeps the mark separate from a dark tab bar.
- Print: 10 mm wide for the mark, 8 mm tall for the lockup.

**Wordmark.** Always lowercase: `plinkfx`. In running text it is also written plinkfx, lowercase, even at the
start of a sentence (rephrase the sentence if that reads badly). "fx" is plink-coloured in the lockup only;
in text it is the same colour as the rest of the word.

### The accent rule

Plink is the sound landing, so it appears where something happens: the drop in the mark, the waveform of the
sound that is playing, the play button while it plays, the focus ring, the caption in demo recordings. It is
never a large background. The two exceptions are the playhead (the playing waveform and play button) and the
one pixel in the mark. A page that is mostly orange has lost the idea.

## 4. Typography

**Wordmark:** custom pixel glyphs, defined as bitmaps in `GLYPHS` in `make_brand.py` (p, l, i, n, k, f, x on a
9-row grid with a descender for "p"). They are not a font and have no other letters. Don't set the name in a
pixel font to imitate it.

**Everything else:** the system monospace stack, as in `gallery/style.css`:

```css
--mono: ui-monospace, "SF Mono", "JetBrains Mono", "Cascadia Mono", Menlo, Consolas, monospace;
```

Why monospace and why the system's own:

- plinkfx is sound as code. Sound names, parameters and commands are code, and they sit next to the prose
  everywhere: in the gallery, the README and the terminal. One typeface for both keeps them in one voice.
- Measurements line up. Loudness, peaks and durations in the gallery and in `plinkfx measure` read as columns.
- No font download. The gallery promises that nothing loads except the page; a web font would break that and
  cost more than the whole 2.5 kB player.

Sizes in the gallery: 15 px base with 1.5 line height. Weights: regular for text, 600 or 700 for headings and
labels. Emphasis is weight or plink-text, not italics.

## 5. Signature sound: plink

The brand has a sound, defined in `library/ui.ts` like every other sound in the library:

```js
plink: ["The plinkfx signature: one drop of sound", {
  verb: [0.35, 0.22],
  vary: 0.3,
  layers: [
    { wave: "sine", freq: 1050, to: 2150, curve: 4, sweep: 0.05, a: 0.001, d: 0.32, shape: 3 },
    { wave: "sine", freq: 3150, fm: [1.41, 1.2, 0.04], a: 0.001, d: 0.12, shape: 4, gain: 0.25 },
    { wave: "triangle", freq: 525, to: 1075, curve: 4, sweep: 0.05, a: 0.001, d: 0.15, shape: 3, gain: 0.25 },
  ],
}],
```

A sine that jumps up about an octave in 50 ms, a faint metallic partial on top, a triangle an octave below for
body, and a short room. It's a water drop with a bit of shine.

Where it plays: the first time someone taps the gallery outside a sound, and when they click the logo. The
logo pings at the same moment. Use it sparingly, once per visit, as a greeting. It is not a notification
sound. Play it with `play("plink")`.

## 6. Voice

- Plain and measured. Say what a sound is and how it was checked ("−16 LUFS, true peak −1.9 dBTP"), not how it
  makes people feel.
- No superlatives without a number behind them. "The smallest" or "the best" only with the comparison shown.
- Name other tools by name and give them credit. ZzFX, jsfxr and Kenney are the references people know.
- Lead with what someone gets: named sounds, matched loudness, readable parameters, CC0, a tiny player,
  engine files. The words "AI" and "procedural" don't belong in headlines.

## 7. Don'ts

- No gradients, glows or drop shadows. Colours are flat; the pixel grid is the only texture.
- No glassmorphism, blur or frosted panels.
- No emoji in headings or labels. (The ▶ on the gallery link is a play glyph, not decoration.)
- No plink as a large background. Only the playhead and the one pixel.
- Don't recolour the drop, rotate the mark, outline it, smooth the steps into a curve, or draw it on anything
  other than paper, ink or a photo dark enough for the paper version.
- Don't put the category swatches on text or use them as section colours.
- Don't add a speaker, headphones or music note to the mark.
